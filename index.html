I study Information Systems and Network Engineering at Chiang Mai University's international engineering school — an English-taught program covering network infrastructure, systems design and information security.

My interest sits at the layer most people never think about: how packets actually reach their destination, and what happens when someone tries to make them go somewhere else. Reading about it only gets you so far, so I learn by building — standing up a lab, segmenting it, attacking it, then fixing what I broke.

Right now that means working toward CCNA, building out a virtualised network lab, and documenting the whole process in public as I go.

lab

building04

Anything I claim to know, I want running somewhere I can break it. This is the architecture I'm standing up — one machine, four segments, deny by default.

fig. 01 — target lab architecture · subject to change as it gets built

The isolated LAB segment is the point of the whole design. Vulnerable machines need somewhere to live where a mistake costs nothing — no route out, no path to anything I care about.

Everything else follows from that constraint: if the risky segment has to be sealed, the rest has to be deliberately segmented too.
